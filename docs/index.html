THE RESEARCH QUESTION

Can one adversarial image
mislead different frontier models?

O-Attack studies this question in the black-box setting. Small image perturbations, optimized on accessible surrogate models, transfer to unseen MLLMs and redirect their responses toward a target scene—even under different prompts.

01 — THE INSIGHT

Look beyond the final layer.

Surrogate models contain a broad, high-level, cross-modally aligned semantic space spanning late visual and textual layers.

Existing transfer attacks typically rely on final-layer surrogate features. Our CKA analysis reveals that alignment extends across a contiguous region of late layers; some non-final representations also show stronger similarity to victim models than final outputs.

This motivates using an aligned subset of late layers for cross-modal optimization, distinct from aggregating representations across the full depth of a single modality.

02 — THE METHOD

Anchor. Explore. Reach consensus.

O-Attack turns this insight into three complementary stages, using frozen surrogate encoders with standard data augmentation and ensembling.

I CSA

Anchor the semantic space

Use CKA trajectories to select stable late visual and textual layers. Project their representations into each surrogate’s shared embedding space.

II PSS

Explore semantic conditions

Progressively widen the range of sampled dropout probabilities, moving from stable alignment to more diverse stochastic surrogate representations.

III SCO

Optimize for consensus

Average alignment scores over anchor layers, then maximize their mean and penalize their variance across surrogate models and augmented views.

Optimization principleHigh target alignment + low disagreement across sampled conditions.Formal formulation ↗

05 — WHAT MAKES IT WORK

Complementary components.
A favorable efficiency trade-off.

Each stage contributes to transferability. On six frontier commercial MLLMs, removing progressive sampling or textual anchoring reduces average ASR.

06 — PRACTICAL IMPLICATIONS

More reasoning is not
a reliable defense.

Increasing inference-time reasoning effort does not consistently mitigate the attack and can amplify the induced semantic errors. Shared vulnerabilities also extend to safety-critical image moderation.

Reasoning effort

On GPT-5.5, high-effort reasoning raises O-Attack’s ASR from 64% to 70% and AvgSim from 0.601 to 0.649, compared with no reasoning.

A concrete failure under high-effort reasoning +

Safety-critical image moderation

On UnsafeBench, adversarial perturbations cause unsafe images to be judged safe. Average detection rates across four categories fall from 76.3% to 9.1% on GPT-5.5 and 65.7% to 1.0% on Claude Opus 4.8.

↗

Transferable visual perturbations expose practical risks in frontier MLLMs, motivating more rigorous robustness evaluation and defenses that go beyond increasing reasoning effort.
